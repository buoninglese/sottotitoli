15 Linguistic Features — Hardcoded Dictionaries

Each can be expressed as a JS lookup table, same pattern as PRON_TYPE. No API calls needed.

01 · Contraction Expansion
Contracted → Full Form
Web Speech API strips apostrophes. Map "cant"→"can't", "lets"→"let's" for display & NLP.
can'tdon'tI'mlet'swe'll
{cant:"can't", dont:"don't", im:"I'm", lets:"let's"}
02 · Irregular Plurals
Singular → Irregular Plural
child→children, man→men, mouse→mice. Useful for vocabulary stats & grammar checks.
child→childrenman→menmouse→mice
{child:"children", man:"men", mouse:"mice", tooth:"teeth"}
03 · Irregular Past Tense
Present → Simple Past
go→went, buy→bought, teach→taught. Complements VERB_TENSE for accurate tense counting.
go→wentbuy→boughtteach→taught
{go:"went", buy:"bought", teach:"taught", speak:"spoke"}
04 · Comparative & Superlative
Base → Comparative → Superlative
good→better→best, bad→worse→worst. Irregular forms that don't follow -er/-est pattern.
good→better→bestbad→worse→worst
{good:{cmp:"better",sup:"best"}, bad:{cmp:"worse",sup:"worst"}}
05 · Article Agreement
a vs an — Phonetic Rule
Map words that start with vowel SOUNDS for correct article: "an hour" not "a hour".
an houran MBAan honest
{hour:"an", MBA:"an", honest:"an", heir:"an"}
06 · Countable vs Uncountable
Mass Nouns — Always Singular
advice, information, furniture, luggage — never pluralized. Flags for grammar feedback.
advice ✗advicesinformationfurniture
{advice:true, information:true, furniture:true, news:true}
07 · Phrasal Verbs
Verb + Particle → Meaning
give up=quit, look after=care for, run into=meet. 5000+ in English. Group by particle.
give uplook afterrun into
{"give up":"quit", "look after":"care for", "run into":"meet accidentally"}
08 · Collocations
Natural Word Pairs
make+a decision, take+a break, heavy+rain. Sound natural vs. "do a decision".
make a decisiontake a breakheavy rain
{decision:["make","take","reach"], break:["take","have"], rain:["heavy","light"]}
09 · False Friends IT↔EN
Italian-English Lookalikes
actually≠attualmente, sensible≠sensibile. Critical for Italian learners of English.
actually≠attualmentesensible≠sensibile
{actually:"in realtà (not attualmente)", sensible:"sensato (not sensibile)"}
10 · Common Prefixes
Prefix → Meaning → Examples
un-=not, re-=again, pre-=before. Helps learners decode unfamiliar words.
un-re-pre-mis-
{un:"not (undo, unhappy)", re:"again (redo, rewrite)", pre:"before (preview)"}
11 · Common Suffixes
Suffix → POS → Examples
-tion=NOUN, -ous=ADJ, -ize=VERB. Already used heuristically in _gvTagWord.
-tion-ous-ize-able
{tion:"NOUN (education)", ous:"ADJ (dangerous)", ize:"VERB (realize)"}
12 · Homophones
Same Sound, Different Spelling
there/their/they're, to/too/two. Flag for spelling checks or vocabulary notes.
there·their·they'reto·too·twohear·here
{there:["their","they're"], to:["too","two"], hear:["here"], write:["right"]}
13 · Formal ↔ Informal
Register Pairs
get→obtain, start→commence, buy→purchase. Useful for academic/business mode.
get→obtainstart→commencebuy→purchase
{get:"obtain", start:"commence", buy:"purchase", help:"assist", need:"require"}
14 · British vs American
Spelling Variants
colour↔color, realise↔realize, centre↔center. Flag for consistency.
colour↔colorrealise↔realizecentre↔center
{colour:"color", realise:"realize", centre:"center", favour:"favor"}
15 · Semantic Word Families
Thematic Groups
Emotion: happy/sad/angry. Movement: walk/run/jump. Size: big/small/tiny. Vocabulary clustering.
emotionmovementsizetime
{emotion:["happy","sad","angry","excited"], movement:["walk","run","jump","slide"]}
